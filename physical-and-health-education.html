About Physical and Health Education

Physical and Health Education teaches students how to maintain good health, develop physical fitness, participate safely in sports, and understand the importance of hygiene and healthy living.

JSS1 Physical and Health Education

1. Meaning and Importance of Physical Education

Meaning

Physical Education is the learning of physical activities, movement skills, games, and exercises that help develop the body and mind.

Importance

  • Improves physical fitness.
  • Develops coordination and balance.
  • Encourages teamwork and discipline.
  • Helps students develop healthy habits.

Revision: Define Physical Education and state three benefits.

2. Physical Fitness and Its Components

Physical fitness is the ability to carry out daily activities effectively without excessive tiredness.

Components include:

  • Strength — ability of muscles to exert force.
  • Endurance — ability to continue an activity over time.
  • Flexibility — ability of joints to move through a range of motion.
  • Speed — ability to move quickly.
  • Agility — ability to change direction quickly and safely.
  • Balance — ability to maintain body stability.

Revision: List five components of physical fitness.

3. Personal Hygiene

Personal hygiene means keeping the body clean to maintain health and prevent illness.

  • Bathe regularly.
  • Brush the teeth morning and night.
  • Wash hands with soap and clean water.
  • Wear clean clothes.
  • Keep fingernails clean and short.
  • Use clean toilets and dispose of waste properly.

Revision: Explain personal hygiene and give four examples.

4. Nutrition and Balanced Diet

Nutrition is the process by which the body obtains and uses nutrients from food.

A balanced diet contains the nutrients the body needs in suitable amounts.

  • Carbohydrates provide energy.
  • Proteins support growth and repair.
  • Fats provide energy and help with some body functions.
  • Vitamins and minerals support good health.
  • Water helps regulate body processes.
  • Fibre supports healthy digestion.

Revision: What is a balanced diet? Name five classes of nutrients.

5. Fundamental Movement Skills

Movement skills are basic actions used in games, exercise, and everyday activities.

  • Walking and running.
  • Jumping and hopping.
  • Throwing and catching.
  • Kicking and striking.
  • Balancing and changing direction.

Practise in a safe space and follow the teacher's instructions.

Revision: Identify four fundamental movement skills.

6. Athletics: Running, Jumping and Throwing

Athletics includes track and field events.

  • Track events include sprints and distance races.
  • Jumping events include long jump and high jump.
  • Throwing events include shot put and javelin.

Warm up before exercise, use the correct equipment, and keep a safe distance from throwing areas.

Revision: Give two examples each of track and field events.

7. Safety in Physical Activities
  • Warm up before strenuous activity.
  • Use sports equipment correctly.
  • Wear suitable clothing and footwear.
  • Drink water when needed.
  • Keep playing areas free of hazards.
  • Report injuries to the teacher or responsible adult.

Revision: State five safety rules for physical activities.

8. Communicable Diseases

Communicable diseases are illnesses that can spread from one person or source to another.

Examples include influenza, measles, and some diarrhoeal diseases.

Prevention methods include handwashing, appropriate vaccination, safe food and water, good ventilation, and avoiding the sharing of personal items where infections may spread.

Revision: What is a communicable disease? State three prevention methods.

9. Posture and Body Care

Posture is the way a person holds the body while standing, sitting, or moving.

  • Sit with the back comfortably supported.
  • Avoid carrying loads that are too heavy.
  • Use both shoulders when carrying a suitable backpack.
  • Change position regularly when sitting for a long time.

Revision: Define posture and state three ways to care for the body.

JSS2 Physical and Health Education

1. Principles of Physical Fitness

Physical fitness can be improved through regular, suitable exercise and sufficient recovery.

  • Regularity: exercise consistently.
  • Progression: increase activity gradually.
  • Specificity: practise activities related to the skill or fitness goal.
  • Recovery: allow the body time to rest.

Revision: Explain progression and recovery in exercise.

2. Warm-Up and Cool-Down

A warm-up prepares the body for activity. A cool-down helps the body return gradually to its resting state.

  • Begin with gentle movement.
  • Use movements suited to the activity.
  • Finish with slower movement.
  • Stretch gently without forcing joints or muscles.

Revision: Give three benefits of warming up before exercise.

3. Team Games and Sportsmanship

Team games require players to cooperate to achieve a common goal.

Examples include football, basketball, volleyball, and handball.

Good sportsmanship includes respecting officials, following rules, cooperating with teammates, and accepting results fairly.

Revision: What is sportsmanship? Name three team games.

4. Football: Basic Skills and Rules

Football is a team game in which players try to score by getting the ball into the opposing team's goal, mainly using their feet.

Basic skills include passing, dribbling, shooting, trapping, and defending.

  • Players must follow the referee's decisions.
  • Fouls may result in free kicks or other sanctions.
  • Outfield players generally cannot deliberately handle the ball during play.

Revision: List four football skills and explain why rules are important.

5. Volleyball: Skills and Rules

Volleyball is played by two teams separated by a net. A team tries to send the ball over the net so it lands in the opposing team's court.

Basic skills include serving, passing, setting, attacking, and blocking.

Revision: Name four volleyball skills.

6. First Aid and Sports Injuries

First aid is the immediate assistance given to an injured or suddenly ill person before professional medical care is available.

  • Make sure the area is safe.
  • Inform a teacher or responsible adult.
  • For serious injuries, seek emergency medical help.
  • Do not move a person when a serious neck or back injury is suspected unless there is immediate danger.
  • Do not give food or drink to an unconscious person.

Revision: Define first aid and list three actions to take when someone is injured.

7. Substance Abuse and Health

Substance abuse is the harmful or improper use of substances such as medicines or other drugs.

It may affect learning, judgement, relationships, and physical health.

Prevention includes making informed choices, following medical instructions, and seeking help from a trusted adult or health professional when concerned.

Revision: State three possible effects of substance abuse.

8. Puberty and Personal Health

Puberty is the stage when a young person's body develops toward physical maturity.

Changes can include growth spurts, body hair, changes in body odour, and emotional changes. The timing and pace vary from person to person.

Good hygiene, adequate sleep, balanced meals, and speaking with a trusted adult about concerns support health during puberty.

Revision: Define puberty and list three changes that may occur.

9. Drug Education and Refusal Skills

Drug education helps students understand the effects and risks of medicines and other substances.

  • Use medicines only as directed by a qualified health professional or the label.
  • Do not accept unknown substances.
  • Practise saying no to unsafe pressure.
  • Seek support from a trusted adult when needed.

Revision: Give three ways to resist pressure to use harmful substances.

JSS3 Physical and Health Education

1. Training Methods and Physical Fitness

Training methods are organised activities used to develop fitness and sports skills.

  • Continuous training: steady activity over a period of time.
  • Interval training: alternating periods of activity and recovery.
  • Circuit training: completing a series of exercise stations.
  • Flexibility exercises: movements that help maintain or improve joint movement.

Training should suit the student's ability and be increased gradually.

Revision: Describe three training methods.

2. Principles of Sports Training
  • Overload: the body needs an appropriate challenge to improve.
  • Progression: training demands increase gradually.
  • Specificity: training relates to the desired skill or fitness goal.
  • Reversibility: fitness may decline when training stops for a long period.
  • Rest and recovery: the body needs time to recover.

Revision: Explain overload and specificity.

3. Athletics and Competition

Athletics includes running, jumping, and throwing events.

Competitions require preparation, correct technique, fair play, and compliance with event rules.

Officials help ensure events are conducted safely and fairly.

Revision: Identify three athletics events and explain the role of an official.

4. Recreation and Leisure

Recreation consists of activities people choose during their free time for enjoyment, relaxation, or personal development.

Examples include walking, dancing, gardening, swimming in supervised settings, and playing games.

Benefits include relaxation, social interaction, and support for physical and mental wellbeing.

Revision: Define recreation and list four recreational activities.

5. Common Diseases and Prevention

Diseases can be infectious or non-infectious.

  • Infectious diseases can be caused by germs and may spread between people or through other routes.
  • Non-infectious diseases do not spread directly from person to person in the same way.

Prevention may include vaccination, clean water, proper sanitation, nutritious food, physical activity, and appropriate medical care.

Revision: Differentiate between infectious and non-infectious diseases.

6. Environmental Health and Sanitation

Environmental health involves keeping surroundings safe and healthy.

  • Dispose of refuse properly.
  • Keep drains and toilets clean.
  • Protect drinking water from contamination.
  • Reduce stagnant water where mosquitoes can breed.
  • Keep classrooms and playing fields free of hazards.

Revision: State five ways to maintain environmental sanitation.

7. Mental Health and Stress Management

Mental health concerns emotional, psychological, and social wellbeing.

Helpful habits include getting enough sleep, staying connected with supportive people, taking breaks, participating in enjoyable activities, and asking a trusted adult or health professional for help when needed.

Revision: List four healthy ways to manage everyday stress.

8. Emergency Response in Sports

Emergency response means taking appropriate action when a serious injury or sudden illness occurs.

  • Check that the scene is safe.
  • Call for a teacher, trained first aider, or emergency medical assistance.
  • Follow the instructions of trained responders.
  • Do not attempt treatment beyond your training.
  • Keep other students away from danger.

Revision: State four actions to take during a sports emergency.

9. Leadership and Teamwork in Sports

Leadership is the ability to guide and encourage others toward a shared goal.

Good sports leaders communicate clearly, follow rules, respect others, encourage teamwork, and act responsibly.

Revision: Mention five qualities of a good sports leader.

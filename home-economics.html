JSS1 Home Economics

1. Meaning and Importance of Home Economics

Home Economics is the study of skills and knowledge that help people manage the home, family resources, food, clothing, and personal well-being.

Importance

  • Teaches good personal and household hygiene.
  • Encourages healthy eating habits.
  • Develops cooking, sewing, and home-management skills.
  • Promotes wise use of money, time, and other resources.
Revision questions
  1. Define Home Economics.
  2. State three benefits of studying Home Economics.
2. The Family and Family Relationships

A family is a group of people related by birth, marriage, adoption, or other recognised family relationships.

Types of family

  • Nuclear family: Usually parents and their children.
  • Extended family: May include grandparents, uncles, aunts, cousins, and other relatives.

Qualities of healthy family relationships

  • Love and respect.
  • Honest communication.
  • Cooperation and responsibility.
  • Care and support for one another.
Revision questions
  1. What is a family?
  2. Differentiate between a nuclear and an extended family.
3. Personal Hygiene

Personal hygiene means keeping the body clean to maintain health and prevent the spread of disease.

Good hygiene practices

  • Bath regularly and wear clean clothes.
  • Brush the teeth morning and night.
  • Wash hands with soap and clean water, especially before eating and after using the toilet.
  • Keep fingernails clean and short.
  • Maintain clean hair and footwear.
Revision questions
  1. Define personal hygiene.
  2. List four good personal hygiene practices.
4. Food and Nutrition

Food provides the body with energy, nutrients, and substances needed for growth, repair, and good health.

Main classes of nutrients

  • Carbohydrates: Main source of energy; examples include rice, yam, and maize.
  • Proteins: Help growth and tissue repair; examples include beans, eggs, fish, and meat.
  • Fats and oils: Supply energy and help the body absorb certain vitamins.
  • Vitamins and minerals: Support normal body functions and health.
  • Water: Helps regulate body temperature and transport substances.
  • Fibre: Helps normal bowel movement.
Revision questions
  1. Name five classes of nutrients.
  2. Give two examples of protein-rich foods.
5. Kitchen Safety and Hygiene

Kitchen hygiene involves keeping the kitchen, food, utensils, and hands clean. Kitchen safety means preventing accidents while preparing food.

  • Wash hands before handling food.
  • Keep raw and cooked foods separate.
  • Use clean utensils and safe water.
  • Store food at appropriate temperatures.
  • Handle knives carefully and turn pot handles away from the edge of the cooker.
  • Clean spills promptly to prevent falls.
Revision questions
  1. State three kitchen hygiene rules.
  2. Mention three ways to prevent kitchen accidents.
6. Clothing and Clothing Care

Clothing protects the body, provides comfort, and may express culture or personal identity.

Ways to care for clothes

  • Read garment-care labels.
  • Sort clothes according to colour and fabric.
  • Wash with a suitable detergent.
  • Dry clothes properly before storing them.
  • Iron only when appropriate for the fabric.
  • Repair loose buttons and small tears.
Revision questions
  1. State three functions of clothing.
  2. Give four ways to care for clothes.

JSS2 Home Economics

1. Meal Planning

Meal planning is deciding in advance what foods will be prepared and eaten over a period of time.

Factors to consider

  • Age and nutritional needs of family members.
  • Available money and food supplies.
  • Food preferences and cultural practices.
  • Seasonal availability of foods.
  • Time available for preparation.
Revision questions
  1. What is meal planning?
  2. State four factors that influence meal planning.
2. Food Preservation

Food preservation uses suitable methods to slow spoilage and help food remain safe and usable for longer.

Methods

  • Refrigeration: Keeping food cold.
  • Freezing: Keeping food at freezing temperatures.
  • Drying: Removing moisture from food.
  • Pasteurisation: Heating certain foods or drinks to reduce harmful microorganisms.
  • Canning: Processing food and sealing it in suitable containers.
Revision questions
  1. Define food preservation.
  2. List four methods of food preservation.
3. Laundry and Fabric Care

Laundry is the washing and care of clothes and other washable fabrics.

Basic laundry steps

  1. Read the care labels.
  2. Sort clothes by colour, fabric, and level of dirt.
  3. Check pockets and treat stains appropriately.
  4. Wash with a suitable detergent.
  5. Rinse, dry, and iron if appropriate.
  6. Fold or hang clothes and store them cleanly.
Revision questions
  1. What is laundry?
  2. Why should clothes be sorted before washing?
4. Consumer Education

Consumer education teaches people how to make informed choices when buying and using goods and services.

Wise buying practices

  • Compare prices and quality.
  • Check expiry dates and product labels.
  • Read instructions and safety warnings.
  • Ask for receipts where appropriate.
  • Avoid counterfeit or unsafe products.
Revision questions
  1. What is consumer education?
  2. List four things to check before buying food.
5. Home Management

Home management is the planning and use of household resources to meet family needs.

Household resources

  • Human resources: Time, energy, knowledge, and skills.
  • Material resources: Money, equipment, furniture, and supplies.

Good home management involves planning, organising, budgeting, and reviewing how resources are used.

Revision questions
  1. Define home management.
  2. Differentiate between human and material resources.
6. Clothing Construction

Clothing construction is the process of making garments from fabric.

Basic tools

  • Measuring tape.
  • Fabric scissors.
  • Needles and thread.
  • Tailor's chalk.
  • Ruler and suitable pattern paper.

Basic stages include taking measurements, preparing a pattern, cutting fabric, sewing, and finishing the garment.

Revision questions
  1. Name four tools used in clothing construction.
  2. List three stages in making a garment.

JSS3 Home Economics

1. Balanced Diet and Nutritional Needs

A balanced diet provides the nutrients and energy a person needs in suitable amounts for health, growth, and activity.

Important points

  • Eat a variety of foods from different food groups.
  • Include fruits and vegetables regularly.
  • Drink safe water.
  • Choose suitable portions and limit excessive salt and added sugar.
  • Consider age, activity, and individual nutritional needs.
Revision questions
  1. What is a balanced diet?
  2. State four features of a healthy eating pattern.
2. Food Spoilage and Food Safety

Food spoilage is a change that makes food unacceptable or unsuitable to eat. Some harmful microorganisms can also make food unsafe without obvious changes in appearance or smell.

Ways to reduce food contamination

  • Wash hands and keep preparation surfaces clean.
  • Use safe water and fresh ingredients.
  • Cook foods thoroughly.
  • Keep raw foods separate from cooked foods.
  • Refrigerate perishable foods promptly.
  • Follow safe storage instructions and discard unsafe food.
Revision questions
  1. Explain food spoilage.
  2. List four ways to prevent food contamination.
3. Family Budgeting

A family budget is a plan showing expected income and how money will be spent over a given period.

Steps in preparing a budget

  1. Identify expected income.
  2. List essential expenses.
  3. Estimate the cost of each expense.
  4. Set aside money for savings where possible.
  5. Compare spending with income and adjust the plan.
Revision questions
  1. What is a family budget?
  2. State four steps in preparing a budget.
4. Interior Decoration and Home Beautification

Interior decoration is arranging and decorating indoor spaces to make them comfortable, attractive, and useful.

Principles to consider

  • Colour: Choose colours that work well together.
  • Lighting: Provide suitable natural or artificial light.
  • Balance: Arrange objects so the room feels visually balanced.
  • Space: Avoid overcrowding rooms with furniture.
  • Cleanliness: Keep the room tidy and well ventilated.
Revision questions
  1. Define interior decoration.
  2. Mention four factors to consider when decorating a room.
5. Family Health and First Aid

Family health involves practices that protect the well-being of family members. First aid is the immediate assistance given to an ill or injured person before professional medical care is available.

General safety practices

  • Keep medicines and chemicals safely stored.
  • Maintain clean surroundings and use safe drinking water.
  • Keep a suitable first-aid kit accessible to responsible adults.
  • Seek help from a responsible adult and emergency services for serious injuries or illness.
  • Do not give medicines unless directed by an appropriate healthcare professional or according to approved instructions.
Revision questions
  1. Define first aid.
  2. State three ways to promote family health.
